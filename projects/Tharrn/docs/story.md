# Progression and Story (2026-10-19)

The owner, 2026-10-19: "you are the story behind the game, how we got here, what we do, quests, progression, raids
ect. you controll the Progression and dungeons tab, name is Progression and Story." This page is the story bible and
the progression design: how we got here, what we do, the factions, the main story act by act, the quest types,
levels and XP, the talent trees, loot and blueprints, dungeons, raids and the endgame. The wiki's **Progression and
Story** tab reads it.

Everything here is **draft 1, a proposal for the owner**, unless it carries a decision number. It keeps every fact
the direction and the GDD already fix (the Exodus Initiative, the convoy and the signal, the four origins, the five
quest types, level 60 and the XP formula, the three trees, the five loot tiers, the dungeon rooms and bosses, the
endgame's raids, faction wars and territory, gates to new regions) and fills the gaps between them. Names are
placeholders the owner can change. Nothing comes from an earlier game project (D-09). Spoilers throughout.

## 1. The story in one page

**How we got here.** Thirty-one years ago the Exodus Initiative's deep-space arrays heard a signal from a star nobody
had looked at twice: a structured pattern that repeated every nine hours and never changed. One sound in it, played
aloud, everybody wrote down the same way: *Tharrn*. The Initiative had been founded to find humanity a second home,
and the signal gave it a direction. It sent a probe, and the probe found an ocean world with one great island, two
planets hanging in its sky, and the signal coming from under the island's rock.

Then it sent people. **Convoy Meridian** reached Tharrn twelve years ago, lost two of its four landers coming down,
built a research station on the great mesa and transmitted for fourteen months. Its last message said the signal had
changed. Then nothing.

**Convoy Lantern** is yours: a carrier and twelve landers, eight hundred operatives (scientists, soldiers, salvage
crews and corporate envoys), sent to find Meridian and answer the signal properly. As the landers came down the island
answered. A pulse from under the rock reached into every system, the ships' electronics failed, and the landers fell
across the island. Each starting location is where one of them hit (D-28). You wake in the wreck of yours.

**What we do.** Survive the crash. Find the other survivors and the Initiative's rally point, the Coastal Outpost.
Secure the extraction beacon, which should call a lander down from the carrier, and find out why nothing that comes
down flies again. Follow Meridian's trail up the mesa and learn what they learned. Go under the island to the Ruins of
the First Signal, where the signal is loudest, and face what guards it. Then decide, with every other survivor on the
server, what to do with what you found: that is the endgame.

**The mystery, in short.** The island is held together by a buried machine: a lattice of crystal conduits through its
rock, which Meridian's survivors call **the Lattice**. Its makers are gone. The survivors call them **the Choir**,
because everything they built sings. The Choir left the Lattice singing the signal: an invitation and an instruction
set, a call for someone to come and take over as its **keeper**. Any ship that answers is pulled in: the Lattice's
landing reflex tries to take the ship's systems in hand, human electronics cannot take the handshake, and the ship
falls. Meridian worked all this out. Its commander tried to answer the call alone, the Lattice's guardians stopped her,
and the survivors went to ground on the mesa and learned to live with the hum. Initiative technology falls silent at every pulse and
nothing it builds can fly through one; technology grown from the Lattice's own crystal is let through: the Alien loot
tier, the Alien Synthesis talent, the nodes under your skin. Whoever answers the song in full becomes the keeper, and the keeper commands the gates that ring the island, the
way to other worlds. That is what the factions come to fight over.

## 3. The island's secrets, act by act

What the player learns, in the order they learn it. Each line is a piece of the mystery with the place it is found.

| Secret | What it is | Where you learn it |

| **The hum** | Near the Lattice's nodes a sound sits just under hearing. It wears on the mind: the neural stability vital. Your crystal node lets you bear it longer than Meridian's crew could | Act I, the first crystal outcrop |
| **The Lattice** | A buried machine of crystal conduits that holds the island together: the crystal fields are where it breaks the surface, the gravity anomalies and the floating islands are its work, the high-gravity plateau is where it strains | Act II, the desert; Act III, the Remnant |

| **The Wardens** | Phase Wardens are the Lattice's guardians, not animals: alloy and crystal, a shield and a step through space. They defend the nodes and the ruins | Act II, the desert |

| **The call** | The signal is an invitation: the Lattice calls for a keeper. The landing reflex that crashes ships is its welcome | Act III, the Ruins of the First Signal |

## 4. Factions

Four factions among the survivors. Each has a hub, a questline, a reputation track and vendors. The vertical slice
builds the Remnant's questline first (it leads into the first dungeon); the others follow in the alpha.

| Faction | Who | Hub | What they want | What they give |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **The Initiative** | Lantern's surviving officers and anyone who still follows orders | Coastal Outpost (built from the landers' hulls) | Order, the beacon network, extraction home, and the call silenced so no one else falls | Missions, armour, ammunition, rescue and escort quests, the first talents' trainers |
| **The Remnant** | Meridian's survivors, eleven years on the mesa, half of them with crystal grown into their skin | Remnant Research Hub (the station on the mesa) | To stay, study the Lattice and live with it; they do not trust the Initiative that left them | Crystal technology, implants, the way into the Choir's ruins, the tech vendors |
| **The Consortium** | The corporations that paid for both convoys, through their envoys and their security contractors | Contested Ruins (a fortified dig), later the archipelago fortresses | The Lattice's secrets and the gates: whoever holds the keeper's seat owns the way to other worlds | Credit, rare blueprints, vehicles, contracts in PvP zones |
| **The Free Camps** | Scavengers, settlers and deserters who answer to nobody | Scavenger Flats (the trading posts where the plains meet the desert) | Land, trade and claims; neither home nor a corporation | Trade, building tiers, bounties, claims in PvP zones |

**Reputation** is a standing with each faction, from quests, deliveries, bounties and events: Stranger, Known, Trusted,
Sworn, Legend. Each rank opens vendor rows and one faction blueprint; Legend gives the faction's signature item. In the
endgame a player swears to one faction for the faction wars (section 12); the other three stop at Trusted.

## 7. Quests

The direction's five quest types, how each works and what the first ones are. Quests are data (stages, objectives,
rewards) run by a server-side quest subsystem (GDD 04 build note); the journal shows objectives, stages, lore entries
and map markers (GDD 10).

| Type | How it works | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Main** | The story's chapters (section 6), one at a time, with cinematics at the act breaks. Rewards: large XP, a blueprint at each act's end, the next region's map | *Beacon*, *Mesa*, *First Signal* |
| **Side** | Found in the world: survivors, wrecks, terminals, a creature's nest. Short, self-contained, repeatable where it makes sense | *Twelve Landers* (find every crash site), *The Black Box* (a lander's log, a lore entry), *Nest Clearing* |
| **Faction** | From the faction's board at its hub; raise reputation; each rank unlocks the next chapter of the faction's own story | Initiative: *Flare Line* (plant beacons between the Outpost and the forest). Remnant: *Tones* (record a Choir door's tones). Consortium: *Dig Site Seven*. Free Camps: *Bounty: the Hollow* |
| **Timed** | Open for a window and then close: the pulse (every nine in-game hours, 22 real minutes with OQ-11's day), a storm, a migration, a hotspot rotation. Rewards above the zone's loot tier | *Bloom* (harvest crystals during a pulse, 10 minutes), *Tidal Storm* (reach the beacon before the storm), *Hotspot* (hold the rotating PvP node) |
| **Dungeon** | Given at a dungeon's door, or by a faction: clear the dungeon, kill its boss, bring back a specific item; the weekly dungeon quest rotates the dungeon and its modifiers | *The Cathedral's Heart*, *Warden Prime*, the weekly |

**Cadence.** Daily missions: three from each hub's board (a kill, a gather, a delivery), refreshed at the server's
midnight. Weekly: the world event (the apex migration or a hotspot rotation), the weekly dungeon, the raid lockout.
Seasonal: a new story chapter, a world event, a gate.

**Data.** `Data/Quests.csv`: `quest_id,name,type,faction,level,giver,prerequisites,stages,rewards`; a stage is
`objective_type:target:count` (kill, gather, reach, interact, craft, deliver, survive_minutes, extract), stages
separated by semicolons; rewards `xp:N;item:ID:count;rep:FACTION:N;blueprint:ID`. Lore entries in
`Data/Lore.csv` (`lore_id,title,text,where`), shown in the journal when found. The subsystem keeps each player's
quest states on the server and replicates only that player's; a group shares kill and reach objectives.

## 8. Levels and XP

Level cap 60, one talent point a level, the direction's curve: `XP(level) = baseXP × 1.12^(level − 1)` is what the
next level costs. **Proposal: baseXP = 100.** The curve is steep: the last ten levels hold two thirds of all the XP to
60, so rewards scale by the same 1.12 a level (below) and every level takes about the same time at content of its own
level.

| Level | XP to the next level | All XP so far |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 100 | 0 |
| 10 | 277 | 1,476 |
| 20 | 862 | 6,353 |
| 30 | 2,675 | 21,458 |
| 40 | 8,308 | 68,391 |
| 50 | 25,804 | 214,142 |
| 59 | 71,601 | 596,000 |
| 60 | cap | 667,600 |

**Sources of XP (proposal).** Every reward below is at level 1 and is multiplied by `1.12^(content level − 1)`, the
creature's, quest's or zone's level, so a level 20 kill is worth 8.6 times a level 1 kill and a level 40 kill 83 times.
With a level's content giving about 50 % of the level's cost an hour, a level takes about two hours, and level 60
about 120 hours of play at level; a group shares kill XP in full.

| Source | XP at level 1 |
|---|---|
| Kill: small, medium, large creature | 5, 12, 30 |
| Kill: boss | 400 |
| Weak-point kill | +25 % |
| Side quest, faction quest, daily | 40, 60, 60 |
| Main chapter | 150 |
| Timed quest | 80 |
| First visit to a zone, a landmark found | 25 |
| First craft of a recipe | 10 |
| Dungeon cleared (its level) | 300 |
| Raid boss | 800 |

**Where the levels happen (proposal).** The zones' recommended levels end at 30 while the cap is 60: the main story
carries a player to about 30, and the endgame (raids, dungeons at higher difficulties, the archipelago, faction wars)
carries them to 60. The tundra, badlands and arctic have no zones yet (the Map chat's layout, 2026-10-19); their
levels are proposals until zones exist.

## 9. Talents

points spent in that tree, and the fifth tier is a choice of one keystone of two. A tree holds 25 points of talents, so

## 10. Loot, blueprints, mastery and reputation

| Tier | Where it comes from | Needs a blueprint? |
|---|---|---|
| **Common** | The coast, the Flats; any creature | No |
| **Uncommon** | The forest, the buffer belts; crafted at a bench | No |
| **Rare** | The Hollow, the caverns' mouth, the Research Hub, the migration path; dungeon chests; faction rank Trusted | Yes, to craft |
| **Advanced** | The Crystal Expanse, the Contested Ruins, the plateau, the Echo Caverns; dungeon bosses; faction rank Sworn | Yes |
| **Alien** | The archipelago; raid bosses; world bosses; Legend; Alien Synthesis | Yes, and an alien core |

**Blueprints** are learned, not carried: once read, the recipe is yours. They come from main chapters (one at each
act's end), dungeon and raid bosses (unique to each), faction ranks, world events and blueprint fragments (five make a
random blueprint of the tier). A server's global boss kills unlock server-wide tech nodes (section 12): blueprints
everyone on the server can read for the season.

**Weapon mastery** is per family (SMG, rifle, shotgun, pistol, heavy, energy), ten ranks each from use: each rank
+1 % handling (reload and aim time), and ranks 3, 6 and 10 give an attachment blueprint for that family (a barrel, an
optic, a core: deliverable 7's attachments).

**Reputation** ranks (section 4): Stranger 0, Known 500, Trusted 1,500, Sworn 4,000, Legend 10,000 standing. A faction
quest gives 50 to 150 standing, a daily 50, a bounty 100, a world event 250.

## 12. Raids and the endgame

**Raids** are instanced, for eight players (two dungeon groups), 60 to 90 minutes, three bosses and a final one,
modifiers rolled weekly, a lockout of one clear a week for each character, Alien loot and unique blueprints. Wipes send
the group to the last cleared boss. The first raid is the beta's; the second a season's.

| Raid | Where | Level | Bosses | In the story |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **The Heart of the Lattice** | Under the lagoon, through the Skyroot | 30, then 40 and 50 | The Choirmaster's Guard (three Wardens as one), the Tide (an Echo Swarm filling the hall), the Apex Matriarch, the Keeper's Seat (the Lattice itself: a puzzle fight on the gravity phases) | The keeper's seat. The first server to clear it sees the first gate open |
| **Lantern** | The carrier in orbit, reached through the first gate | 40, then 50 and 60 | Lander bay automatics, the bridge's Warden, the hum made flesh in the cold-sleep hall, the captain | What the Lattice did to the carrier, and the convoy's command still up there |

**World bosses.** The apex migration (Apex Migration Path, a world event once a week): the herd crosses the west coast
under the Apex Matriarch; killing her is a global boss kill, and the server unlocks a tech node (a blueprint everyone
can read) for the season. Later events: a Warden host at a pulse, a tidal storm's leviathan.

**Faction wars and territory.** The archipelago's fortresses and the Contested Ruins are control points, captured by
flagged players of a sworn faction (PvP rules, GDD 05). The holding faction's members pay less at its vendors and get a
share of the zone's rare nodes; control resets weekly, and the season's winner names the next chapter's villain.

**Seasons and gates.** A season is three months: a story chapter (the keeper's choice carried one step further), a
world event, a dungeon at a new difficulty, cosmetic rewards. An expansion opens a gate to a new region, a map
extension reached by the gate or by a long-range vehicle (GDD 01 §1.6), and the story goes with it.
