State of the design

What was decided, what is still open, what the story currently claims and what is built: the facts to write the game's two pages against.

Where it stands

Tharrn is eight days old. It was created on 2026-10-15, the day it replaced Wildwright as the only active game (D-01), from one direction document the owner wrote that day: 2,353 words, six sections, the design source (D-02). In the week since, the chats wrote GDD 0.1 in thirteen modules, a story bible of thirteen sections, a decision log, a task list and five engineering records, and built the prototype's first five modules in C++.

The honest reading of those files: the fiction is coherent but nobody signed it, the foundations are still open questions in the log, and production ran ahead of both. The code does not depend on the fiction, so none of the technical work is lost whichever way the story goes.

Where the build drifted from the direction

  • The map grew thirty times across three decisions, from 16 to 25 km² to 714 km², while the 45-minute crossing from your direction stayed on paper as a target (D-19) and the real crossing became 2.5 hours (OQ-23).
  • The name changed from Aetherfall: Exodus to Tharrn (D-17), and the lore grew from one sentence in your direction to a 38 KB story bible that carries its own question in the log (OQ-25).
  • The build order left the roadmap. Section 6.1 asked for core shooter feel, vitals, one biome, three creatures and a MetaHuman base. After P5 the chats decided five weapon tiers (D-37, D-38), four building tiers (D-33), hand tools (D-39), a 3D generation route for the arsenal (D-40), a radial build menu (D-44) and a restyle of the whole interface (D-31, D-36), each as a decision, while scope (OQ-07) stayed open.
  • The decisions outran the owner. Many decisions in the log record "as proposed" on a chat's recommendation rather than a choice you made first; the open questions below are the ones the chats could not close that way.

What the story currently claims

The story bible's one page, in short. Thirty-one years ago the Exodus Initiative heard a structured signal from a star nobody had looked at; one sound in it everybody wrote down the same way: Tharrn. A probe found an ocean world with one great island and the signal coming from under its rock. Convoy Meridian went twelve years ago, lost two of its four landers, transmitted for fourteen months from the great mesa, said the signal had changed, and went silent. Convoy Lantern is yours: a carrier, twelve landers, eight hundred operatives. As the landers came down a pulse from under the rock took every ship's electronics; the landers fell across the island, each starting location is where one hit (D-28), and you wake in the wreck of yours.

The island is held together by a buried crystal machine the Remnant call the Lattice, left singing the signal by its makers, the Choir, as an invitation and an instruction set: a call for someone to come and take over as its keeper. The ending is a choice made with every other survivor on the server about what to do with it.

FactionWho
The InitiativeLantern's surviving officers and anyone who still follows orders
The RemnantMeridian's survivors, eleven years on the mesa, half of them with crystal grown into their skin
The ConsortiumThe corporations that paid for both convoys, through their envoys and their security contractors
The Free CampsScavengers, settlers and deserters who answer to nobody

The acts and milestones: the prototype plays Act I's Wreck; the vertical slice adds Beacon, the Remnant's questline (Act III's Mesa and First Signal, with the Apex Titan) and the first extraction; the alpha fills Acts II and IV with the other factions; the beta builds Act V and the raid. All of this is the Story chat's proposal of 2026-10-19. The log still lists the story itself as a question for you (OQ-25).

Identity and rules: the only active game, the direction as source, the new project and its repository, no content from earlier games, the name, a PC game (D-01 to D-09, D-13, D-17, D-42). What is still open

Two kinds of question wait on you. The foundations: scope (OQ-07), the story (OQ-25), the endgame's shape (OQ-27), where players start (OQ-16), travel across the new map and how its zones fit it (OQ-23, OQ-24), consoles and dedicated servers (OQ-01, OQ-02). The rest are technical or content questions the chats can carry once the foundations are set: hit detection, anti-cheat, the ocean, the terrain, the tutorial's first fight, the XP curve, the vehicles' speeds, the title screen, the building pieces. What exists and survives any change of story

  • 148 C++ files in the game module and its editor module, in the folders Building, Combat, Data, Inventory, Net, Player, Progression, Survival and Tests. The last clean build was 2026-10-22.
  • P1 Foundation: data tables from CSV files, 31 automation tests, the multiplayer test harness (listen server, two clients, dedicated server) and a grey test range.
  • P2 Shooter character: the motion-matched player with sprint, slide, mantle and vault, the movement rules on the server so the game holds up under lag.
  • P3 Weapons: shots predicted on the player's machine and decided on the server, with hit zones, armour, falloff, recoil curves and bodies rewound to the moment of the shot.
  • P4 Inventory, hotbar and UI: twelve hotbar slots, a thirty-slot grid, worn slots, a weight limit, items and the starting kit from CSV files, the server owning all of it.
  • P5 Vitals and crafting: stamina, hunger, hydration, felt temperature and neural stability, run on the server, and the first recipes.
  • P6 Coastal slice: a 1 km test coast with the bundle's jungle, Unreal's ocean and sky, measured against the frame budget.
  • Base building: four tiers, the piece factory, the gallery level, server-placed pieces, corner posts, a build tool and a radial menu under way.
  • Story track: the quest, quest stage, lore and progression tables and their row types, Act I's first chapter on the test range.

Only names bind this work to the fiction: weapon rows, tier labels, faction and quest text in CSV files. Renaming is cheap; the systems do not care who the Choir were.

The two pages only you can write

  • The premise in one paragraph, in your words. The chats have one; the question is whether it is yours.
  • The player fantasy: who the player is, and what they feel doing the loop for an hour.
  • The core loop in one line, and what one session of thirty to sixty minutes looks like.
  • Three pillars. Keep, change or replace the five the GDD lists: elite gunfeel, high-stakes survival, deep RPG, consensual danger, an extensible world.
  • How it ends: the endgame's shape and the ending, in a paragraph (OQ-27, and the last act of OQ-25).
  • What is cut for the first playable: the answer to scope (OQ-07), as a list of what is not in it.

Write these before deciding whether Tharrn stays. They are the cure whichever game they describe.
