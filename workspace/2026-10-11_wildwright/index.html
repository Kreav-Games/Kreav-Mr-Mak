The repeatable loop

Observe. Change. Return.

Observe→Form a hypothesis→Cast an element or combination→Read the result→Record knowledge→Use the new option→Revisit a changed region

Knowledge opens useful options. The cast is the performance; the changed ground, route or habitat is the consequence.

Four forces.
Clear consequences.

Shared target tags, bounded state and deterministic resolution. Three effects are accepted; three still need implementation.

What ecological memory stores
  • Repeated overharvesting lowers local vegetation health and increases recovery time.
  • Water diversion changes moisture distribution; one route may green while another dries.
  • A burned patch follows a recovery stage rather than snapping back to baseline.
  • Creature territory suitability changes when water or food patches change.

Spectacle contract

Five rules for every cast.

  1. The cast layer is allowed to be as cinematic as the budget permits.
  2. The cast layer reads from the state layer and requests changes through the rule resolver. It never mutates world state itself.
  3. Every meaningful result must also be readable after the effect ends, via a material change, mesh state, sound or journal entry (see 5.4).
  4. Disabling all VFX must leave gameplay state identical (test in Appendix A.2).
  5. A spectacle feature may not be accepted if a tester cannot say what it did.
