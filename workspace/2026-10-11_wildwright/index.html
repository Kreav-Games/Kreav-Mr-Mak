Read the land→Choose an intervention→Shape it→Watch the immediate response→Let the world respond→Return and read the consequence→Adapt

Scars, not resets · GDD 4.1

The valley remembers.

  • Players may burn, soak, cut, raise and redirect.
  • The world remembers selected consequences; nature applies recovery pressure through regrowth, recolonisation, weather and succession.
  • Recovery does not have to recreate the original. Changed conditions can produce a new stable state: a drained bog becomes meadow, a flooded hollow becomes marsh.

Earthworks · GDD 4.7

Sculpt, stored compactly.

  1. Define a path. Earth targeting places points along the ground; the preview follows the terrain.
  2. Validate. Every segment on soft ground; slope within limits; depth not into bedrock; no crossing of protected geology; intersections with other earthworks resolved (a channel through a berm breaches it); within the earthwork budget
  3. Generate geometry. A channel or berm profile is swept along the spline and conforms to the terrain.
  4. Sell it. Exposed soil, blended edges, displaced stones, vegetation removed along the path, dust and debris, sound and animation.
  5. Feed the systems. Collision, navigation, the flow network (§4.8), cell material and moisture, and vegetation (cleared cells start at Bare).
  6. Persist compactly. Spline points, profile, width, depth, material, connections and creation time; rebuilt on load.

Water · GDD 4.8

A network decides; the surface follows.

  • Flow network. Nodes (springs, junctions, basins, outlets) joined by links (stream reaches, channels). Each link has a bed elevation at each end and a capacity from its cross-section.
  • Sources inject flow (a spring gives, for example, 100 units). Basins store water up to their volume, then overflow. Outlets remove it.
  • Distribution. Flow at a node splits among outgoing links in proportion to capacity times elevation drop. Water never runs uphill and is never created: the sum leaving a node equals what entered, minus what a basin stores.
  • Earthworks change the network. A channel adds a link when one end meets water and its bed falls downhill along its length; a berm closes the links it crosses. A barrier or a new ditch can shift a 70/30 split to 20/80.
  • Recompute happens when the network changes, never per frame; it visits each link a bounded number of times, so loops cannot run forever.
Fire · GDD 4.9

Fuel, dryness, wind and slope.

  • Ignition. ER_FIRE_IGNITE on a cell or object with enough fuel and low enough moisture.
  • Spread. Each step, a burning cell heats its neighbours; a neighbour ignites when its heat passes a threshold set by its fuel and moisture, with wind and uphill slope lowering the threshold downwind and upslope.
  • Burn-out. Fuel is consumed; the cell becomes Smouldering, then out; burn severity records the scar; vegetation drops to Scorched (§4.10).
  • Stopping fire. Wet cells, channels and bare ground carry no fire; ER_WATER_WET extinguishes; ER_AIR_GUST can turn the front.
  • Presentation. Niagara flames, smoke and embers near the player; far away the burn is a regional number with cheap distant smoke.

Spectacle contract

Five rules for every cast.

  1. The cast layer may be as cinematic as the budget allows.
  2. The cast layer reads state and requests changes through the rule resolver. It never writes world state itself.
  3. Every meaningful result must stay readable after the effect ends: terrain, water, vegetation, material, sound or a journal entry (§5.4).
  4. Disabling every visual effect leaves gameplay state identical (Appendix A).
  5. A spectacle feature is rejected if a tester cannot say what it did.

MVP-1 acceptance · GDD 14.5

The technical proof.

  1. A realistic grassy stream bank above a lower basin.
  2. Earth (debug input in MVP-1) draws a curved trench across valid soil.
  3. A convincing channel forms, with collision and removed, blended vegetation.
  4. The system detects that the channel connects to the stream and runs downhill.
  5. Visible moving water runs through the new channel.
  6. Flow in the original route drops; ground downstream of the new route gets wetter.
  7. An earth berm blocks or redirects the flow again.
  8. Save, exit, reload: the trench, water route and environmental state come back.
  9. CPU, GPU, memory and save-data cost are measured before more complexity is added.
